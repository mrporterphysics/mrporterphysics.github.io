Average Velocity Is Not Instantaneous Velocity

Why Δx/Δt from a position–time table never gives you the speed at the times in the table

The situation

A cart rolls down a ramp with uniform (constant) acceleration. A motion detector records its position every 0.20 seconds. You are asked:

“Use the data to find the velocity of the cart at t = 0.40 s, t = 0.80 s, and t = 1.20 s.”

The obvious move is to subtract neighbouring positions and divide by the time between them. That answer is always wrong — and it is wrong by exactly the same amount every single time. This page shows why, and what the number you calculated actually is.
