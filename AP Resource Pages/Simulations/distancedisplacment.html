Position, Distance & Displacement

Big Question

You walk from your desk to the classroom door, 6 m away, and then walk straight back to your desk. How far did you walk? Where are you now? How much did your position change? These sound like the same question, but physics gives them three different answers using three different words: position, distance, and displacement. Use the simulations below to figure out how they differ.

Position

symbol: x  ·  vector  ·  unit: m

Where an object is, measured from a chosen reference point (the origin). The sign tells you which side of the origin it is on.

“Where are you right now?”

Displacement

symbol: Δx = xf − xi  ·  vector  ·  unit: m

The straight-line change in position from start to finish, including direction. Only the start and end matter, not the path in between.

“How far, and which way, did you end up from where you started?”

Distance

symbol: d  ·  scalar  ·  unit: m

The total length of the path actually traveled. It has no direction, is never negative, and never goes down while you move.

“How much ground did you cover?”

  • Distance is always greater than or equal to the size of the displacement: d ≥ |Δx|.
  • They are equal only when the object moves in one straight line without turning around.
  • Returning to where you started always gives a displacement of zero, no matter how far you traveled.
  • To combine legs of a trip: add displacements with their signs; add distances as plain sizes.
