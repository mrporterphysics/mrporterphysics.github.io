Speed vs. Velocity: Average & Instantaneous

Guiding Question

A speedometer reads 0 m/s, yet the car had an average speed of 12 m/s on the trip. A runner finishes a 400 m lap with an average velocity of zero. How can these both be true?

Use the simulation to tell apart speed and velocity, and the average and instantaneous versions of each. Choose a motion, pick a time interval (t₁ to t₂) and an instant (t), and watch how the five quantities are found on the motion diagram, the position–time graph, and the velocity–time graph.

Speed vs. Velocity: the big idea

Speed scalar

How fast something moves. Magnitude only, never negative. Built from distance (d), the total length of the path traveled.

Velocity vector

How fast and in which direction. The sign (+ or −) gives the direction along the x-axis. Built from displacement (Δx = xf − xi), the straight-line change in position.

Each one comes in two versions: average (over a time interval Δt) and instantaneous (at a single moment, the limit as Δt → 0).

Key asymmetry: Instantaneous speed is always the magnitude of instantaneous velocity. But average speed is not generally the magnitude of average velocity. They match only when the object never reverses direction during the interval.

Check your understanding

Predict first, then use the simulation to check.

1. In “Walk out, pause, walk back,” what are the average velocity and average speed for the whole trip (0–10 s)?

Δx = 4 m − 0 m = +4 m and d = 12 m + 8 m = 20 m. Average velocity = +4 m ÷ 10 s = +0.40 m/s; average speed = 20 m ÷ 10 s = 2.0 m/s. The walker reversed direction, so average speed is larger than |average velocity|.

2. In “Ball rolled up a ramp,” when is the instantaneous speed zero? What is the average velocity from 0 to 10 s?

The speed is zero at t = 5 s, the top of the ramp, where the tangent line on the x–t graph is horizontal. The ball returns to x = 0, so Δx = 0 and average velocity = 0 m/s, even though average speed = 30 m ÷ 10 s = 3.0 m/s.

3. In “Constant velocity cart,” why are all four speed/velocity readouts the same (ignoring sign)?

The x–t graph is a straight line, so every secant and every tangent has the same slope, and the cart never reverses, so d = |Δx|.

4. Can average speed ever be less than the magnitude of average velocity?

No. Distance traveled can never be less than the straight-line displacement, so d/Δt ≥ |Δx|/Δt.

5. Pick any motion and press “Shrink Δt → 0.” What happens to the secant line and to the average velocity?

The secant line rotates until it lies on top of the tangent line, and the average velocity approaches the instantaneous velocity at t. That limit is the definition of instantaneous velocity.

6. Does a car’s speedometer show average speed, average velocity, instantaneous speed, or instantaneous velocity?

Instantaneous speed: it reads how fast the car is going right now and has no direction (it reads the same driving north or south, and it would not go negative in reverse).
