WHAT THIS DECK IS: the projection companion to the packet "Guided Notes: Ellipses and Eccentricity" (ess-u1-guided-notes-ellipses.pdf). Slides follow the packet Part for Part. Students fill their blanks as each slide comes up. WHY IT EXISTS: it is the bridge into the NYS investigation "Unearthing Mars" (HS-ESS1-4), Thu 11/19 and Fri 11/20. Every part aims at steps 13-16 of its Student Directions. WHAT IT DOES NOT REPEAT: the orbits deck already did foci, major axis and e = f/d. This picks up from there. If students have not seen that deck, run its Part E first. EVERY BLANK ANSWER is shown in gold. Advance, let students write, then discuss. TIMING: about 35 minutes at pace, leaving the block's second half for the practice packet.

PACKET: Part A, the opening two blanks. RUN IT LIVE IF YOU CAN. Two pins, a loop of string, a pencil, one volunteer at the board. Thirty seconds of watching beats any slide. THE POINT TO LAND: the shape is not drawn freehand and it is not a squashed circle. It is the consequence of one rule.

PACKET: Part A, the "One string, one major axis" box. THIS IS THE SLIDE THAT MAKES ECCENTRICITY FEEL PHYSICAL. Eccentricity is not a number someone looked up — it is how far apart you put the pins. ASK: "If I want a rounder orbit, do I change the string or the pins?" The pins. Every time.

PACKET: questions 1 and 2. WATCH FOR: students who change d when the pins move. The string did not change, so d did not change. That is the whole trick of the question. QUESTION 3 (the string shorter than the pin gap) is worth asking aloud: the pencil cannot reach, because the shortest route between the pins is the straight line between them.

PACKET: Part B, the table and the diagram. PERI = NEAR, AP = AWAY. Worth saying once; the Greek does the memorising for them. BOTH DISTANCES ARE MEASURED FROM THE SUN, never from the center. That is packet question 6 and it is the one students get wrong under time pressure.

PACKET: Part B, the two identities and the "Why this matters in the state lab" box. THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT SLIDE IN THE DECK. Step 14d of the investigation is exactly this subtraction, and it is where marks are lost. WHY THE SUBTRACTION WORKS: the Sun is off center by half of f on each side, so the long piece exceeds the short piece by exactly f. Draw it on the board if they look unsure.

PACKET: the Part B worked example, three blanks. PACE: do this one with them, then set question 4 (aphelion 15.3, perihelion 12.7 → e = 0.093) cold. That answer is almost exactly Mars, and noticing it is the point.

PACKET: question 5. TEACH THIS AS A HABIT, not a question. Every student who runs this check in the lab catches their own ruler error before it becomes a 40% deviation. COMMON CAUSES of the disagreement: the ruler did not pass through the Sun, or it stopped short of the curve.

PACKET: Part C, the formula box. MAKE THEM FIND IT IN THE ESRT NOW, not on lab day. Students who have never located the equation on the page lose two minutes hunting for it under lab conditions.

PACKET: Part C, five blanks plus the precision rules. POINT 2 IS THE ONE WORTH DWELLING ON. A pencil drawing on a desk can be compared with a planet 228 million km away, because eccentricity is a ratio. That is not a trick — it is why the model works at all. PACKET QUESTION 9 makes it concrete: two students draw the same orbit at different sizes, 3.0/30.0 and 4.5/45.0, and both get 0.100.

PACKET: Part D, three blanks. THE DIAGRAM IS HONEST: the two shaded sweeps really do have equal area, to within 0.1%. Students can check it by eye and it holds up. NOT NEEDED FOR THE MARS LAB. This is the natural cut if the block is running short — but the practice packet uses it in questions 5 and 15.

PACKET: questions 10 and 11. THIS IS ALSO WHY MARS GAVE KEPLER THE ANSWER. Its orbit is stretched enough for the discrepancy to show in Brahe's data — Earth's or Venus's would have hidden it.

PACKET: Part E, three blanks. PERCENT DEVIATION AND PERCENT ERROR ARE THE SAME THING. The ESRT says deviation; the investigation says error. Say so once so neither word stops them.

PACKET: the worked example, then questions 12-14. QUESTION 13 IS THE REASONING ONE: the partner who says the accepted value must be wrong. Four centuries of observation against one pencil drawing — the deviation measures our procedure, not the planet. QUESTION 14: we divide by the ACCEPTED value so everyone is measured against the same reference and results can be compared.

PUT THIS IN FRONT OF THEM NOW. A student who spots the clash mid-lab stops working and asks; a student who has seen this slide shrugs and carries on. NEITHER SOURCE IS WRONG. Mars' eccentricity is about 0.0934 and the two tables round it differently. That is worth one honest sentence about significant figures.

THE CLOSER. Say the last line out loud — it is the one that lowers the temperature before a state investigation. WHAT IS GENUINELY NEW IN THE STATE LAB: triangulating Mars' position from paired observations. The measuring and the arithmetic are already theirs.

PACKET: the closing terms. THE THREE RELATIONS ON THE BOTTOM LINE ARE THE WHOLE SHEET. A student who can write those three from memory can do the state lab.
